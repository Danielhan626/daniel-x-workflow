# X Cover Standard

Apply this before any registered visual style whenever an image serves as an X Article header, shared-Article preview, or the opening image of a post/thread. This is Daniel's editorial acceptance standard, not a claim that X mandates a particular art direction. A body screenshot, chart, or evidence image keeps its evidentiary purpose and need not become a teaser cover.

## The Three-Second Job

At feed size, one glance should tell the intended reader: "This concerns me, and there is a concrete reason to open it." Legibility and relevance outrank polish. Define the reader, the exact article payoff, and one visual focal point before choosing a style. If the hook could sit on any unrelated article, rewrite it.

## Placement And Crop

- Distinguish a regular Post's image from an Article header and its shared timeline preview. X says a single Post photo in the 2:1 to 3:4 aspect-ratio range can display in full, but this is **not** a guarantee for an Article header or every share surface. X's Article guidance does not publish one fixed universal header crop. Do not use a profile-banner size or an anecdotal very wide header measurement as the cover specification.
- For an Article cover, start with a horizontal composition. Center the subject and essential hook inside a conservative middle safe area; make all four edges expendable. Do not park words, faces, logos, or proof in corners. Check both wide header and small feed crops in the current X editor/preview. If one master image cannot survive both, export separate header and sharing variants where the editor permits, or simplify until it can.
- A portrait style reference is a mood reference, not an export preset. A standalone single-photo Post may legitimately use a portrait image; do not mislabel it as an Article-cover-safe file.
- Leave space around the subject. A person's gaze or gesture should lead inward or toward the hook. If a real product, place, or person is the subject, show it clearly rather than hiding it behind atmospheric effects.

## Hierarchy And Restraint

1. **One focal point:** one face, product, contrast, or meaningful number. A before/after or two-position image counts as one *relationship* only when the difference is immediately clear; reduce competing labels and decorations.
2. **One readable hook:** preferably one line, usually no more than 6-12 Chinese characters when Chinese is used. In other languages, fit the same small-card reading test rather than translating that count literally. State a concrete result, conflict, or reader question; avoid vague mood words and claims unsupported by the article. Omit a subtitle unless it is essential. Put steps, criteria, caveats, and evidence in the post or Article body.
3. **Strong contrast:** dark-on-light or light-on-dark must remain clear after reduction, compression, and in dark mode. Do not make pale gray-on-white or text-over-busy-photo the primary hook.
4. **Recognizable system:** use at most two type families and roughly three intentional design colors (natural photo colors do not count). Reuse a consistent 1-2-font/one-accent/layout skeleton across a series; vary the actual subject, not a template gradient. Avoid excess glow, shadows, stickers, and tiny UI labels.
5. **Choose one structure:** large type on a quiet field; one strong relevant image plus a short hook; or a clean before/after, right/wrong, or with/without comparison. A cover is not an infographic.

For `daniel_cinematic_poster` only, the named preset defines a three-role type system (Chinese brush headline, optional English high-contrast serif, modern sans information) and a black/gray/cool-white base with red emphasis and subordinate muted blue. This is an explicit exception to item 4, not a change to other styles. Its headline remains the single dominant focal point; the keyword, portrait and short CTA are subordinate. Follow its 1.91:1 canvas and safe-area rules in addition to these crop/readability checks.

Prefer a clear, relevant authorized photo or screenshot when it communicates the subject better. An abstract visual must have a recognizable shape and strong contrast, not just fog or colored light. Keep cover copy distinct from an Article title when duplication wastes the frame, but never trade clarity for novelty.

## Export And Acceptance

- Export in sRGB. Prefer PNG for type-led graphics and high-quality JPEG for photo-led images; keep files reasonably small and verify the current upload control accepts the final file. X's help currently lists JPEG/PNG/GIF and a 5 MB limit for Post photos; check the live editor for the actual placement rather than applying that number blindly to Articles.
- Inspect the actual exported image at a phone-feed width, in a wide Article-header crop, and against light and dark UI. Check exact wording, source truth, rights, and alt text. A desktop full-size mockup is not acceptance evidence.
- Ask: (1) Is the hook readable when small? (2) Without the title text outside the image, is the payoff clear? (3) Does the essential subject survive center cropping? (4) Does the hook stand out in dark mode? A **no** to any of these means revise and recheck.
- Then ask: (5) Does it still look recognizably Daniel's if another account used the topic? (6) Is there only one clear focal point? (7) Is it helping the reader decide to open, rather than displaying design skill? A **no** here calls for simplification or a stronger recurring visual system.
